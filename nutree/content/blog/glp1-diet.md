## Why Your Diet Matters So Much on GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 Treatments

GLP-1 medications work by mimicking natural gut hormones that regulate hunger and blood sugar. As appetite decreases, it becomes easier to eat less — but also easier to under-eat the nutrients your body still needs.

Clinical research published in *The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology* (2024) found that up to **30–40% of total weight lost** through these therapies may come from **lean body mass (e.g., muscle)** **when diet and physical activity aren’t optimized.** That kind of loss can reduce metabolism, energy, and long-term sustainability.

At Nutree Clinic, our goal is not just weight loss — it’s **metabolic health, muscle preservation, and vitality**.

## Understanding the Medications — and Why Nutrition Must Adapt

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

**Examples:** Semaglutide (Wegovy®, Ozempic®), Liraglutide (Saxenda®)<br>These medications mimic a gut hormone (GLP-1) that increases satiety and slows digestion. The result: smaller portions, fewer cravings, and smoother blood-sugar control.

However, with less overall food intake, maintaining muscle and micronutrient balance becomes essential.

### GIP/GLP-1 Dual Agonists

**Example:** Tirzepatide (Mounjaro®, Zepbound®)<br>This next-generation option activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, improving glucose metabolism and fat loss even further. Studies show it may help preserve lean mass slightly better than GLP-1 alone — but only when paired with a **protein-rich diet and consistent movement**.

## The Core Principles of Eating Well on Weight-Loss Medications

### 1. Prioritize Lean Protein

Protein is your body’s best defense against muscle loss. It maintains metabolic rate, supports recovery, and helps keep you fuller longer — a key advantage when appetite is low.

**Goal:** 1.2–1.5 g of protein per kg of body weight daily (≈ 80–100 g for most adults)**<br>Sources:** lean meats, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, legumes, or protein shakes**<br>Timing:** spread protein evenly throughout meals to optimize absorption and satiety

At Nutree Clinic, our nutrition specialists design balanced meal plans that make hitting your protein goals effortless — even for patients with reduced appetite.

**Examples:**

- ½ chicken breast (≈ 26 g protein / 130 cal)
- ¾ cup non-fat Greek yogurt (≈ 17 g protein / 90 cal)

### 2. Choose Whole, Nutrient-Dense Foods

A **Mediterranean-inspired eating pattern** — rich in vegetables, fiber, healthy fats, and lean proteins — complements GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 medications beautifully. It supports heart health, gut balance, and satiety without deprivation.

**Key foods:**

- Leafy greens and vegetables (spinach, zucchini, green beans, brocoli, carrots, squash...)
- Whole grains like quinoa, farro, and oats
- heathy fats such as Olive oil, nuts, and seeds for hormone support
- Legumes for additional fiber and plant protein

### 3. Don’t Over-Restrict Calories

Appetite suppression can make it easy to under-fuel, which may slow metabolism or cause fatigue.

- Avoid diets below 1,000 cal/day unless prescribed
- Stay tuned to your body’s energy levels
- Drink water throughout the day
- Remember: consistent nourishment leads to better results than extreme restriction.

Your Nutree Clinic provider will help adjust both medication dosing and nutrition to keep your progress safe and sustainable.

### 4. Protect Micronutrients and Hydration

Lower food intake means you must be intentional about vitamins and minerals.

**Pay attention to:**

- **Calcium & Vitamin D** — for bone and muscle support
- **Magnesium, B12, Iron** — for energy and metabolism
- **Electrolytes & hydration** — especially if experiencing slower digestion

Your Nutree Clinic provider may recommend targeted supplementation and periodic lab testing to ensure optimal nutrient balance.
